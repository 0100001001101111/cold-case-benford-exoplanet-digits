# COLD CASE AST-09 — Exoplanet periods lean the same digit-direction in every survey, and that is exactly the problem

Family: astronomy  |  Disposition: **REVIVABLE_WITH_NAMED_DATA**  |  claim_authority=NONE
Members: BENFORD-PERIODS-FACILITY-SCHEMA-RECOVERY-V5

## The case
Confirmed exoplanet orbital periods depart from Benford's digit law - overall, in transit surveys, and in radial velocity, all in the same direction. A telescope-independent digit anomaly would be extraordinary; a selection-effect fingerprint would still be a nice methods result.

## How it died
Every branch failed at least one load-bearing selection-control requirement; imaging and microlensing fell below inferential minimums; and direct prior work already covers the broad result (rediscovery). Informative null, correctly refused promotion.

## What still stands
The same-direction raw departures as a bounded observation awaiting its control. Ceiling: descriptive.

## What would revive it
An external survey-specific completeness model - detection probability as a function of period per facility - or a prospective later-archive cutoff frozen now.

## Card fields (G18 contract)
- plain_meaning: Planet orbit numbers are non-random in their leading digits in a consistent way, and until someone models what each telescope could have detected, that pattern cannot be separated from the way we find planets.
- what_it_is_not: Not numerology confirmed, because selection effects are the favored explanation and remain untested; and not numerology refuted, because the same-direction structure across independent methods is genuinely unexplained at the control level.
- next_decisive_test: The frozen digit test conditioned on any published per-survey completeness model.
- what_we_need: Survey completeness/detection-probability models from Kepler, TESS, and RV teams - published or shareable.
